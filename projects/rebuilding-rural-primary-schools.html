Rebuilding Rural Primary Schools

District📍 Kailahun District
Period🗓 2024–2026
CategoryEducation
Author✍ Ibrahim Sesay
Status● Active

Reconstructing 18 damaged primary schools with solar lighting, latrines and safe learning environments for 4,200 pupils.

The Problem: Schools in Name Only

In Kailahun District in eastern Sierra Leone — one of the poorest districts in the country — a survey conducted by CBN and the Ministry of Basic and Senior Secondary Education in 2023 found that 18 primary schools were operating in conditions that posed a direct risk to the safety and learning of their pupils. Roofs had collapsed, walls were crumbling, latrines were overflowing or absent, and none had any form of electricity.

In total, 4,200 children — of whom 52% were girls — were attending these schools. Teachers reported conducting lessons under mango trees when rain made the classrooms unusable. In some schools, children shared a single textbook between five pupils. In the absence of separate female latrines, many older girls stopped attending school during menstruation.

What We Are Building

The project is reconstructing all 18 schools to a standard design approved by MBSSE, with each school receiving:

  • Three permanent classrooms with cement floors, corrugated iron roofs and proper ventilation
  • A solar lighting system — four panels, a battery bank and LED lighting in all classrooms, enabling evening adult literacy classes
  • Separate sex latrines: 3 stalls for girls (including a menstrual hygiene management space), 2 for boys and 1 for teachers
  • A rainwater harvesting tank (5,000 litres) for handwashing and cleaning
  • A dedicated headteacher's office and a small library room stocked with 200 books
  • A lockable storeroom for teaching materials

All construction uses local contractors and locally sourced materials where possible, creating employment in the communities.

Progress

As of March 2026, 15 of the 18 schools have been fully completed and handed over to their communities. The three remaining schools are expected to be complete by June 2026.

  • 🏫 15 schools completed and operational
  • 👧 Girls' enrolment across completed schools is up 34% compared to the previous academic year
  • 📚 Pupil-to-textbook ratio improved from 5:1 to 1:1 in all completed schools
  • 🔆 All 15 completed schools have functional solar lighting
  • 🤝 Local contractors employed on the project earned a combined Le 2.4 billion in wages

Teacher's Perspective

"I have been teaching at this school for eleven years. For the first five, I taught in a room where the roof had holes and the children could not hear me when it rained. Now I have a classroom, a light, and books. My pupils come to school excited. That is everything."
— Mr. James Lahai, Class 5 Teacher, Njaluahun Primary School, Kailahun

Partners & Funding

Implemented in partnership with MBSSE and the Kailahun District Education Office. Funded by UNICEF Sierra Leone and the Rotary Foundation. Total budget: $720,000. Secured: $612,000 (85%).
