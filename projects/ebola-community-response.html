Background: Sierra Leone and the 2014–2016 Ebola Epidemic

The West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016 was the largest in history. Sierra Leone was among the three worst-affected countries, recording 14,124 confirmed cases and 3,956 deaths. Beyond the direct mortality, the epidemic devastated healthcare systems, shut schools for nine months, collapsed agricultural supply chains and triggered an economic contraction of 21%.

In the early months of the outbreak, a critical obstacle was community resistance. Rumours, fear, distrust of health workers and deeply held cultural practices around burial meant that many families hid sick relatives, continued traditional washing of the dead and fled affected villages — all of which accelerated transmission.

CBN's Response

In August 2014, CBN rapidly retrained and deployed 200 community health mobilisers drawn from within the affected communities — people who were already trusted by their neighbours. The decision to use insiders rather than external health workers proved transformative. Each mobiliser was trained in a three-day intensive programme covering:

  • Ebola transmission routes and clinical signs
  • Safe burial guidance and how to sensitively discuss it with families
  • Rumour identification and evidence-based counter-messaging
  • Referral pathways to Ebola Treatment Units (ETUs)
  • Basic personal protective measures
  • Psychosocial first aid for affected families

Mobilisers conducted household visits — averaging 10–15 houses per day — as well as community meetings, mosque and church outreach and market sensitisation. They operated across all 16 districts at peak deployment.

Scale & Reach

  • 🏠 40,000+ households visited with life-saving information
  • 📢 1,200 community meetings held across all 16 districts
  • 🕌 Outreach delivered in 380 mosques and churches
  • 📞 6,800 hotline referrals logged and followed up
  • ⚰️ CBN mobilisers facilitated 214 safe burials in communities that had initially refused them

Outcomes

An independent evaluation commissioned by WHO and conducted by the London School of Hygiene & Tropical Medicine attributed a 35% reduction in community transmission in districts where CBN mobilisers were active, compared to comparable districts without community-based mobilisation. In post-response surveys, 84% of respondents in CBN-covered areas said they would "definitely" use the ETU if they showed symptoms — up from 31% at the start of the response.

A Mobiliser's Account

"People were so afraid. They thought the government had brought Ebola. When I went to a household, they sometimes chased me away. But I kept going back, with patience. By the third visit, they were asking me questions. I watched people in my own community survive because they came to the ETU in time. That is what I will always remember."
— Alhaji Sesay, CBN Community Health Mobiliser, Kenema District

Legacy

The 200 community health mobilisers trained during the Ebola response formed the foundation of CBN's permanent Community Health Worker network, which today operates in 30 chiefdoms across Sierra Leone. The systems, training materials and community trust built during the crisis have since been used for COVID-19 response, malaria bed-net distribution and maternal health outreach. The Ebola response demonstrated, conclusively, that community-led approaches are not just more ethical — they are more effective.

Partners

WHO Sierra Leone, UNICEF, MSF (Médecins Sans Frontières), Ministry of Health and Sanitation and the Sierra Leone Red Cross. Fully funded by the Ebola Response Multi-Partner Trust Fund. Total expenditure: $620,000.
