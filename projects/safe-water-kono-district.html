Safe Water for Kono District

District📍 Kono District
Period🗓 2024–2026
CategoryHealth & WASH
Author✍ Fatmata Bangura
Status● Active

Constructing 24 solar-powered boreholes to provide reliable clean water to 12,000 people in remote villages.

The Challenge

In Kono District, over 60% of rural households historically walked more than four kilometres daily to collect water from unprotected streams and seasonal ponds. Women and girls bore the heaviest burden — losing four to six hours every day that could have been spent in school, building a business, or caring for family. Water-borne diseases including cholera, typhoid and diarrhoeal illness accounted for nearly a third of under-five child deaths in the district.

What We Are Doing

The Safe Water for Kono District project is installing 24 solar-powered boreholes across 12 rural villages, serving an estimated 12,000 people. Each borehole is fitted with a submersible solar pump, a 5,000-litre overhead storage tank and multiple tap-stands positioned to minimise walking distance to under 300 metres for every household.

Every community receives a 12-week training programme covering:

  • Borehole maintenance and minor repairs
  • Hygiene promotion and handwashing behaviour change
  • Financial management for a community water fund
  • Record-keeping and monthly usage reporting

Each village elects a five-person Water Management Committee (at least three members must be women) that collects a small monthly fee from households — typically the equivalent of $0.30 — to cover spare parts and future repairs. This community-ownership model is the single biggest driver of long-term sustainability.

Progress So Far

By March 2026, 17 of the 24 boreholes have been drilled and are operational. More than 8,500 people now have reliable access to safe water within a 5-minute walk. The remaining seven boreholes are under construction and are expected to be completed by October 2026.

  • 📉 Reported cases of diarrhoeal illness in participating villages are down 61% year-on-year
  • 🏫 School attendance among girls aged 10–17 is up 23% because they no longer spend mornings fetching water
  • 💧 17 Water Management Committees fully trained and operational
  • ♻️ 100% of committees collecting the community water fund on schedule

A Voice from the Ground

"Before the borehole, I woke up at 5 am every morning and walked with my daughters to the river. Sometimes the water was green. Now the tap is five minutes from our door. My daughters go to school every single day."
— Mama Kadi, Njala Village, Kono District

Partners & Funding

This project is implemented by CBN in partnership with WaterAid Sierra Leone and the Kono District Council, with funding from USAID under the Water, Sanitation and Hygiene (WASH) for Rural Communities programme. Total project budget: $480,000. Funding secured to date: $345,600 (72%).

How You Can Help

Each borehole costs approximately $20,000 to drill, equip and commission. A contribution of $5,000 covers the solar pump and tank for one village. Every donation, large or small, brings safe water closer to the remaining 3,500 people still waiting.
