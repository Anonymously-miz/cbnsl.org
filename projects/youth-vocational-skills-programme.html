Free carpentry, tailoring, ICT and agri-business training for 300 out-of-school young people in Southern Sierra Leone.

Sierra Leone's Youth Employment Crisis

Sierra Leone has one of the youngest populations in the world — more than 60% of the country is under 25. Yet youth unemployment in urban and peri-urban areas runs at an estimated 70%, and many young people who are "employed" are in precarious, low-income informal work. For those who dropped out of school — often due to poverty, conflict with family, or pregnancy — formal employment is essentially inaccessible.

In Bo and Kenema, the two largest cities in southern Sierra Leone, CBN estimates that approximately 15,000 young people aged 16–28 are neither in education, employment nor training. This is not a skills problem alone — it is also a confidence problem, a network problem and an access-to-finance problem. Our programme addresses all four.

The CBN Skills Hub Model

The programme operates through the newly opened CBN Skills Hub in Bo — a purpose-built training facility with four workshops, a computer lab, a design studio and a business development room. A second hub is under development in Kenema. The programme enrols 300 young people (at least 50% women) in five six-month training tracks:

Track 1: Carpentry & Joinery (60 trainees)

Furniture construction, doors and window frames, roof truss assembly and basic building work. Sierra Leone's construction sector is growing at 8% annually — skilled carpenters are in high demand.

Track 2: Fashion & Tailoring (80 trainees)

Garment construction from pattern to finished product, both traditional Sierra Leonean styles and contemporary fashion. Graduates are supported to establish their own micro-enterprises or join established tailoring cooperatives.

Track 3: ICT & Digital Skills (60 trainees)

Practical computer literacy, Microsoft Office, basic web and graphic design, social media for business and mobile money operations.

Track 4: Agri-business & Food Processing (60 trainees)

Value-added agricultural processing (palm oil, rice milling, vegetable drying), business management, quality control and market access. Links directly with our Women's Farming Cooperatives programme.

Track 5: Electrical Installation & Solar (40 trainees)

Domestic wiring, solar panel installation and maintenance, and battery system management. As Sierra Leone expands off-grid solar access, trained electricians are acutely scarce.

Beyond Technical Training

Every trainee, regardless of track, receives:

  • Business skills: Basic bookkeeping, pricing, marketing and customer relations
  • Financial literacy: Savings, mobile money, avoiding predatory lending
  • Life skills: Communication, conflict resolution, gender equality and rights
  • Starter pack: Tools or equipment relevant to their trade, valued at $150–$300
  • Business development grant: A competitive pitch at graduation — the top 20% of each cohort receives a $300 start-up grant
  • Alumni network: Ongoing peer support, monthly meetups and access to CBN's market-linkage contacts

Where We Are Now

The programme launched in January 2026. The first cohort of 120 trainees across four tracks is currently mid-programme. The second cohort (180 trainees, including the Kenema hub) is scheduled to begin in August 2026. Funding for the first cohort is secured; additional funding is urgently needed for the Kenema expansion and the second cohort.

  • ✅ 120 trainees enrolled in Cohort 1, Bo Skills Hub
  • 🔨 Carpentry workshop producing furniture for local NGO offices (generating training income)
  • 👗 Tailoring students have completed and sold 240 garments at Bo market
  • 💻 ICT graduates have collectively earned Le 8.4 million from freelance digital work in the first three months

Mohamed's Story

"I was selling mobile phone credit on the street for two years after I left school. I had no future. CBN gave me six months of tailoring training and a sewing machine. Now I have my own shop and I employ three of my friends from the programme. I have a future now."
— Mohamed Kamara, 22, Tailoring Graduate, Cohort 0 Pilot, Bo
