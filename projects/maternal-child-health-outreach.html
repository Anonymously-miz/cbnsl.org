Mobile clinics and 60 trained community health workers reducing maternal mortality in 30 rural chiefdoms.

The Crisis: Maternal Mortality in Pujehun

Sierra Leone has one of the highest maternal mortality ratios in the world — approximately 443 deaths per 100,000 live births (UNFPA, 2023). Pujehun District in the south of the country consistently records figures above the national average. The leading causes are post-partum haemorrhage, eclampsia, sepsis and obstructed labour — all of which are preventable or treatable with skilled attendance at birth and timely referral.

The fundamental problem is distance. Pujehun's 30 chiefdoms are spread across difficult terrain, with many villages more than 30 kilometres from the nearest government health facility. Women in labour may travel for hours by foot or motorbike before reaching care. Many do not make it in time.

Our Approach

The project tackles the distance problem with a two-pronged strategy: bringing care to the community, and ensuring the community is equipped to get women to higher-level care when needed.

60 Trained Community Health Workers

CBN has recruited and trained 60 women — two per chiefdom — as Community Health Workers (CHWs). Each completed a 12-week intensive programme covering:

  • Antenatal care: registering pregnant women, monitoring weight, blood pressure and foetal position
  • Recognising danger signs in pregnancy and labour
  • Clean and safe delivery assistance for uncomplicated births
  • Newborn care: immediate breastfeeding, thermal care, cord care
  • Post-natal follow-up visits at 24 hours, day 7 and day 28
  • Family planning counselling and contraceptive provision
  • Malaria prevention, treatment and bed-net distribution
  • Child immunisation tracking and defaulter tracing

Each CHW is equipped with a standardised kit including a blood pressure cuff, foetal stethoscope, clean delivery kit, rapid diagnostic tests for malaria and a mobile phone for data entry and referral communication.

Monthly Mobile Outreach Clinics

A CBN mobile clinic — staffed by a midwife, a nurse and a CHW supervisor — visits each of the 30 chiefdoms once per month, providing antenatal and post-natal consultations, safe delivery for women in early labour, child growth monitoring and nutrition screening, immunisation and family planning.

Emergency Referral System

CBN has negotiated agreements with 12 motorbike ambulance operators and three vehicle owners in the district, who are on-call 24/7 for obstetric emergencies. A revolving fund of $8,000 subsidises emergency transport for women who cannot pay — no woman is denied transport due to cost.

Early Results (Year 1, 2025)

  • 🤱 1,840 antenatal consultations conducted in the first year
  • 👶 312 safe deliveries attended by trained CHWs or at mobile clinics
  • 📉 Maternal deaths in covered chiefdoms: 2 in 2025, compared to a projected 11 based on historical rates
  • 💉 Child immunisation coverage in Pujehun has risen from 41% to 68%
  • 🏍️ 47 emergency referrals completed using the motorbike ambulance network

Fatmata's Testimony

"In two years we have delivered 312 babies in this chiefdom and we have had only two mothers die. Before the programme, we lost that many in one season. I know the names of every pregnant woman in my area. That is my job and I am proud of it."
— Nurse Fatmata Koroma, Community Health Worker, Pujehun District

Partners & Funding
