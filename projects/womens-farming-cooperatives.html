The Context: Women & Agriculture in Northern Sierra Leone

Women produce approximately 70% of Sierra Leone's food, yet they own less than 10% of agricultural land and receive under 5% of available agricultural credit. In the Northern Province — covering Bombali, Tonkolili, Koinadugu and Falaba districts — most female farmers work on smallholder plots of less than one hectare, growing rice, cassava, groundnuts and vegetables primarily for household consumption.

Without access to better seeds, storage facilities, market linkages or finance, these women are trapped in subsistence farming regardless of how hard they work. The result is household food insecurity, child malnutrition and persistent poverty — even in fertile land with good rainfall.

Our Approach: The Cooperative Model

CBN's Women's Farming Cooperatives project supports 40 women-led cooperatives across the Northern Province, each with 15–30 members, totalling approximately 1,000 women. Each cooperative receives a phased programme over three years:

Year 1 — Foundation

  • Legal registration of the cooperative and opening of a group bank account
  • Training in cooperative governance, record-keeping and conflict resolution
  • Agronomy training: improved seed varieties, soil health, intercropping and composting
  • A starter micro-grant of $500 for seed and basic tools

Year 2 — Growth

  • Post-harvest handling and storage: reducing loss from the current 35% average to below 10%
  • Market linkages: connecting cooperatives to local traders, school feeding programmes and urban markets in Makeni and Freetown
  • Access to a matching grant of up to $2,000 for equipment (drying racks, storage bins, small-scale processing)
  • Financial literacy and introduction to mobile money savings

Year 3 — Sustainability

  • Business planning for expansion and diversification
  • Peer mentoring: established cooperatives mentor newer ones
  • Introduction to formal microfinance institutions for future credit access

Early Results (Year 1, 2025)

With 14 cooperatives now completing their first year:

  • 🌾 Average crop yield among participating farmers is up 42% compared to their own baseline
  • 💰 Average monthly household income increased from Le 320,000 to Le 580,000 (+81%)
  • 🏦 12 cooperatives now have active savings accounts with a combined balance of Le 48 million
  • 🥗 Reported child meal frequency increased from 1.8 to 2.6 meals per day in participating households

Isata's Story

"Before CBN, I farmed alone and sold a little at the roadside. Now our cooperative supplies vegetables to the school feeding programme in three chiefdoms. I have employed two women from my own village. My children eat properly and my eldest daughter is in secondary school."
— Isata Conteh, Cooperative Chair, Tonkolili District

Partners & Funding
