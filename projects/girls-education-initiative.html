Why Girls' Education in Sierra Leone?

Sierra Leone has one of the highest adolescent pregnancy rates in the world, and pregnancy remains the leading cause of girls dropping out of secondary school. In Bombali and Bo districts, just 38% of girls who start secondary school complete it. The barriers are well-documented: school fees, the cost of uniforms and materials, distance to school, domestic workload, and early marriage.

Research consistently shows that every additional year a girl stays in school increases her lifetime earnings by 10–12%, reduces the probability that her own children will be malnourished, and increases the age at which she first gives birth. Educating girls is the single highest-return investment Sierra Leone can make.

What the Initiative Provides

The Girls' Education Initiative supports 1,500 secondary-school girls across Bombali and Bo Districts with a comprehensive package:

  • Scholarship grant: Full annual school fees paid directly to the school on the girl's behalf — no cash in hand that can be redirected
  • Material support: School uniform, textbooks, exercise books and sanitary supplies at the start of each term
  • Mentorship: Each girl is paired with a trained female mentor — a teacher, health worker or community leader — who meets with her monthly
  • Safe Spaces: After-school clubs in 18 partner schools providing a safe, gender-responsive environment for study, discussion and peer support
  • Family engagement: Quarterly meetings with parents and guardians to address attitudes about girls' education and early marriage
  • Emergency fund: A small fund for girls facing sudden household crises (illness, bereavement) that would otherwise force them to drop out

Results Since 2023

The initiative began its first cohort in September 2023 with 500 girls. The second cohort of 500 joined in 2024, and the third cohort of 500 in 2025, bringing total enrolment to 1,500.

  • 🎓 96.4% retention rate across all three cohorts — compared to a district average of 62%
  • 📈 Average exam scores for scholarship recipients are 18% higher than the district average
  • 💬 1,200 parent-guardian meetings held since launch
  • 🏆 12 scholarship girls have won district or national academic awards
  • 👩‍⚕️ Of the 2023 cohort now in their final year, 67% intend to pursue nursing, teaching or public health

Mariama's Story

"When my father died, my mother could not afford the school fees. She told me I would have to stay home and help with my younger brothers. The CBN scholarship changed everything. I completed secondary school and I am now studying nursing at Njala University — the first in my family to go to university."
— Mariama Koroma, 19, CBN Scholar, Class of 2025, Bombali District
