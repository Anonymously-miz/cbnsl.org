What 24 Boreholes Really Mean for Kono

📅 8 March 2026✍ By Fatmata Bangura⏱ 2 min read

Our WASH lead visits Njala village, where the new solar borehole is quietly transforming daily life.

Seventeen of the twenty-four planned boreholes are now operational, bringing safe water within a five-minute walk for more than 8,500 people in Kono District. Each one is fitted with a solar pump and a 5,000-litre tank, and each is owned and maintained by a village Water Management Committee — at least three of whose five members must be women.

Reported diarrhoeal illness is down 61% year-on-year and girls' school attendance is up 23% — because nobody's morning starts with a four-kilometre walk to the river any more. "Before the borehole, I woke up at 5 am every morning and walked with my daughters to the river," Mama Kadi of Njala told us. "Sometimes the water was green. Now the tap is five minutes from our door."

The remaining seven boreholes are under construction and due by October 2026. Each costs roughly $20,000 to drill, equip and commission. Help us reach the last 3,500 people →
