Why Sierra Leone Needs Locally-Led Development

📅 2 March 2026✍ By Aminata Kamara⏱ 2 min read

CBN's Executive Director makes the case for shifting funding and decision-making power to national NGOs.

Less than 2% of international humanitarian funding reaches local and national organisations directly. Yet it is local organisations that stay when the crisis headlines fade, that speak the language, and that are accountable to the community. During the Ebola epidemic it was community health mobilisers from inside the affected villages — not outside experts — who finally broke through fear and rumour and cut transmission by a third in the districts where they worked.

Shifting decision-making power — not just sub-contracts — to Sierra Leonean NGOs is the single most effective reform donors could make. That means multi-year core funding rather than project-by-project grants, locally defined indicators of success, and trusting the people who will still be here in ten years to decide what their communities need in the next one.

At CBN we try to practise what we ask of others: every Water Management Committee is majority-women and village-elected, every cooperative is registered in its members' names, and every programme budget is published. Localisation is not a slogan for us — it is how the work gets done.
